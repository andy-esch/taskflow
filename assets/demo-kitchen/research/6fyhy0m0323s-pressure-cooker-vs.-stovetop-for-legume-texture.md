# Pressure cooker vs. stovetop for legume texture

## Question

For the dal work, is a stovetop pressure cooker's speed worth a texture
trade-off against a long open-pot simmer — or does pressure actually give a
*better* result for some lentils and not others? The instinct going in was
"pressure cooker for speed, open pot for quality," but that's a guess, not a
measurement.

## Findings

Ran side-by-side batches of toor dal (same soak time, same water ratio,
same salt) — one at 3 whistles in a stovetop pressure cooker (~12-15 minutes
at pressure plus natural release), one at a bare simmer in an open pot for
45 minutes.

- **Toor dal:** pressure cooking gave a rounder, more uniformly broken-down
  texture. Sealed, high-heat steam seems to hydrate the lentil evenly all
  the way through, where the open pot's slower heat let the outer layer
  overcook slightly before the center caught up.
- **Water loss:** the open pot needed roughly 1.5x the starting water topped
  up over the cook, since it's not a sealed system — worth accounting for in
  any open-pot recipe or the dal comes out thinner than intended.
- **Masoor dal (a quick side comparison, not the main test):** cooks down so
  fast on the stovetop (18-20 minutes at a simmer) that pressure cooking
  barely saves time and risks overshooting into total mush, since there's no
  way to check texture mid-cook under pressure.
- **Dal makhani's whole urad + rajma** wasn't part of this test — anecdotally
  it seems to want the opposite: the long open simmer is doing real
  flavor-building work (tomato reduction, fat rendering into the lentils)
  that a 45-minute pressure shortcut skips past, not just a texture call.

## Recommendation (as of 2026-08-10)

Default to the pressure cooker for split lentils that fully break down
anyway (toor, moong, masoor) — it's faster and the texture holds up fine,
often better. Reserve the open pot for whole-lentil, slow-built dishes like
dal makhani where the simmer time itself is part of what's being cooked, not
just a means to soft lentils. Revisit if a specific dal (sambar's toor base,
for instance) shows a flavor difference under closer tasting.
