# High-hydration dough behavior with rice flour

## Question

The Vietnamese baguette's shattering crust reportedly comes from working
rice flour into the dough alongside a fairly high hydration (~70%) — but
rice flour carries no gluten. Before committing a full bake day to it: does
rice flour actively weaken the dough's structure at that hydration, and if
so, how much is too much before the loaf can't hold shape through proofing
and scoring?

## Findings

Mixed three small test doughs at a fixed 70% hydration, varying only the
rice-flour share of total flour weight: 0% (control), 10%, and 20%.

- **0% control:** normal baguette dough — good gluten development, held
  shape well through a windowpane test.
- **10% rice flour:** windowpane test still passed, though the membrane
  teared slightly earlier than the control. Dough handled normally through
  shaping. This matches the low end of what recipes for this style tend to
  call for.
- **20% rice flour:** gluten development visibly weaker — windowpane test
  failed (tore immediately), and the dough spread rather than held its
  shape during bench rest. Too much dilution of the gluten network at this
  hydration.
- Rice flour also behaves differently in the final proof: doughs with any
  rice flour relaxed and spread a bit faster than the control during the
  last 20 minutes before baking, suggesting proof time may need trimming
  slightly as the rice-flour share goes up, not held constant.

No steam/bake test was run in this pass — this was purely a mixing and
proofing structure check, done before spending a full bake day on it.

## Recommendation (as of 2026-08-12)

Target 10-15% rice flour of total flour weight at ~70% hydration for the
first full bake attempt — inside the range that still holds a windowpane and
handles normally through shaping, rather than the 20% that visibly failed
structurally in this test. If the crust isn't shattering enough at that
share once steam is dialed in, step up in small increments (2-3% at a time)
rather than jumping straight to 20%.
