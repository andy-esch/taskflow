# Mashed potatoes: ricer vs food mill vs hand mash

## Objective

Compare a ricer, a food mill, and a hand masher on the same russet batch to
see which gives the smoothest texture without overworking the starch — a
ricer or mill shears the cell walls apart cleanly, while a hand masher (or
worse, a food processor) ruptures cells and releases free starch that turns
the mash gluey. Butter and warm milk get folded in gently after ricing, not
beaten, to keep the same starch problem from showing up at the finishing
stage.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Same potato batch split three ways for a fair comparison
- [x] Ricer and food mill both tested, not just assumed better
- [x] Gluey-texture check — none of the three methods over-worked the starch
- [x] Butter-to-milk ratio settled and folded in gently, not beaten

## Out of scope

- Brown-butter or garlic-infused variants — plain mash technique first.
