# Bake the crackly rice-flour baguette

## Objective

The Vietnamese banh mi roll's shattering, paper-thin crust comes from rice
flour worked into an otherwise standard baguette dough (roughly 10-15% of
total flour weight) plus a high-hydration mix around 70%. Rice flour has no
gluten, so it weakens the crumb structure just enough to crisp brittle
instead of chewy on cooling, while the wheat flour still carries the rise.
Getting the balance wrong in either direction either collapses the open
crumb or leaves the crust tough instead of glassy.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Dough hydration held near 70%, rice flour at 10-15% of total flour weight
- [ ] Bulk ferment gives visible strength (windowpane test passes) despite the
      weakened gluten network
- [ ] Crust shatters audibly on cooling rather than staying chewy
- [ ] Crumb stays reasonably open, not dense from too much rice flour

## Out of scope

- Sourdough/levain version — starting from commercial yeast first.
